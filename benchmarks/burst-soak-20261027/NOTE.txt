Sustained-load peak probe: 0.98 and 0.94 side by side, 75 minutes each, same host, same shapes.

WHY THIS EXISTS
The runtime-growth bound that the 0.98 default was qualified on had been measured only at 0.94,
where the remainder above the reservation is ~5.2 GiB. Quoting that number against the 1,395 MiB
the 0.98 default leaves was my error: it presented a growth rate measured with 4x the slack as a
headroom statement about the configuration we ship. This campaign measures it at the shipped
utilization, and under load that is sustained rather than a single excursion, because fragmentation
and leaks are trajectories and a one-shot phase cannot see them.

WHAT IT SAYS
  098/  card 0, GPU_MEMORY_UTILIZATION=0.98, 25 soak rounds, 2,520 samples, control PASS
  094/  card 1, GPU_MEMORY_UTILIZATION=0.94, 29 soak rounds, 2,514 samples, control PASS

  baseline (boot+warmup)        95,496 MiB   91,568 MiB
  structured (FSM decode)       95,986 (+490) 92,058 (+490)
  prefill-240k                  95,986 (+0)   92,058 (+0)
  deep-4x-concurrent            95,986 (+0)   92,058 (+0)
  all-hands                     95,988 (+2)   92,060 (+2)
  decode-16 (graph bucket max)  95,990 (+2)   92,062 (+2)
  every soak round thereafter   95,990 (+0)   92,062 (+0)

  net growth over baseline: 494 MiB at BOTH utilizations, to the MiB, and flat from the first
  round to the last. Zero preemptions in either arm.

THE THREE THINGS THIS ESTABLISHES

1. The growth is a single deterministic step, not a creep. 494 MiB appeared once, on the
   constrained-decode (FSM) path, and 25 further rounds of structured + 4x deep-prefill + vision
   moved it by 0 MiB. That also withdraws the "1 boot in 4" impression from the earlier probe set:
   those runs all plateaued at 92,058-92,082 MiB whether the step appeared at `structured` or was
   already inside `baseline`, so the step was always the same and only its position in the timeline
   varied. Attribution varied; the event did not.

2. The step does not come out of the remainder, which is the correction that matters for the
   default. The plateau sits 61 MiB above the 0.98 budget (95,929 MiB) and 48 MiB above the 0.94
   budget (92,014 MiB). So weights + KV pool + live activations + the 494 MiB step all fit inside
   what the engine reserved for itself, at both utilizations, because the profiled activation
   reserve (1,290 MiB, from engine-accounting.txt) lives inside the budget. Raising the utilization
   did not shrink the space growth needs. The 1,395 MiB remainder is what the engine did NOT use.

3. The sampler can see growth at this scale, on this boot. Each arm ends with a deliberate
   allocation sized from the memory actually free at that moment: 608 MiB held on the 0.98 card
   (1,352 MiB free) registered a 1,163 MiB rise; 1,536 MiB held on the 0.94 card (5,272 MiB free)
   registered 2,091 MiB. The rise exceeds the holding because the caching allocator takes whole
   segments. Either way the flat line above is a measurement, not a blind instrument.

LIMITATIONS, STATED
  * The sampler resolves at 2 s. A growth event shorter than the interval would be missed; the
    control demonstrates sensitivity to sustained allocations, not to transient ones.
  * Two engines on one host were soaking simultaneously. That shares CPU and the NVMe path, which
    is why nothing in this directory should be read as a latency result. It is irrelevant to peak
    device memory, which is the quantity under test.
  * 75 minutes is a soak, not a deployment. A leak with a period longer than ~50 rounds is outside
    this window, though the zero slope over 25 rounds at constant KV occupancy (0.667) bounds it
    at well under the ~1,334 MiB of remainder the step left untouched (1,395 MiB measured by start.sh, of
    which the plateau consumed 61 MiB).

FILES
  peak.tsv               one row per phase/round: peak MiB, delta over previous peak, sample count,
                         preemptions, peak KV occupancy, pool pages.
  samples.txt            the raw 2 s series (epoch, MiB used) the peaks are computed from.
  engine.log             the engine's own startup accounting, including the pool geometry.
  cache-config.txt       vllm:cache_config_info as served: num_gpu_blocks, block_size, util.
  engine-accounting.txt  the "Desired GPU memory utilization / peak activation / Available KV cache"
                         lines, which is where the 1,290 MiB profiled activation reserve comes from.
  soak-deep4-N.jsonl     per-round workload row (wall seconds, preemptions, KV occupancy) -- the
                         trajectory the wall-clock claim rests on.
